A day here

The same shape every day. Dogs settle faster when they can guess what happens next.

  1. 6.45am

    Garden and greetings

    Back door open, everyone out, kettle on. Medication given before food.

  2. 7.30am

    Breakfast, separately

    Your own food, your own bowl, in your own spot. Nobody eats near anyone else.

  3. 9.00am

    The long walk

    An hour on Ham Hill or the Parrett Trail. Off-lead only with your written say-so.

  4. 12.30pm

    Quiet hours

    Curtains half-drawn, radio low. Puppies and older dogs nap in the snug.

  5. 4.00pm

    Second walk and garden games

    Shorter loop through the lanes, then scent games in the paddock.

  6. 8.30pm

    Supper, photo, bed

    Your evening photo and a line about the day. Beds are in the kitchen and the snug — never crated unless you ask.

Who's actually looking after them

Centre Barks Hereford is our home on the edge of Montacute — a cottage with a walled paddock, a wood burner the lurchers refuse to move away from, and two of us here all day. We don't take dogs while we're out at work, and we don't board more than six at a time, which is the number we can genuinely walk, watch and know.

If your dog is nervous, reactive on lead, elderly or on medication, say so when you enquire. We board those dogs often. We'd rather turn a booking down than take one we can't do well.
